Key Insight 3 of 3

Practice creates competence, and competence creates confidence

Within the classroom
CSCE 201: Introduction to Computer Security
Beyond the classroom
NSF REU, University of Alabama
Concept
Cryptographic primitives & sealed-bid auctions

My research journey began on April 7th, 2025, during spring semester of my sophomore year when I was granted acceptance into a National Science Foundation (NSF) Research Experience for Undergraduates (REU) at the University of Alabama. I had spent months applying to countless jobs and research opportunities hoping that something would work. My goal at the time was simple; get a job. The job had finally come, research involving "Security for Emerging Networks in Energy and Intelligent Transportation Systems". I was extremely thankful to have this opportunity but had no idea what to expect. In my personal statements, I mentioned interest in cybersecurity, yet I had no idea what I was getting myself into. When I arrived in Tuscaloosa a week after the spring semester ended, I was shocked to meet my roommate for the summer and learn he was computer science and cognitive neuroscience student at Brown University (hoping to join a Ph.D program at MIT after graduation). Boy did I feel like an imposter, but I was ready for the challenge. The first 2 weeks our cohort was taught the fundamentals of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and various cryptographic primitives. I learned some of the basics like hashing and encryption from CSCE201: Introduction to Computer Security but to be honest, wasn’t captivated by the teacher so didn’t spend the time to fully understand it. The summer soon changed that though. I learned that I would be contributing to research on a decentralized data marketplace auction system. This was perfect. My interests have always been primarily in business, and I guess money, so this was the perfect opportunity to apply technology to create a fair and secure data auction system.

After listening to the first 2 weeks of learning seminars, I was now ready to begin my research. I was the only intern/student working on my project, and I was paired with a graduate student, Hassan, as well. While at this point I now had some understanding of the basics, I now needed to understand how to apply these basics to the problem. To do this, I was given an older paper, “Succinctly verifiable sealed-bid auction smart contract” (Galal & Youssef, 2018) to read and get an understanding of the past work and how we can make improvements. I read this paper 50 times (I’m not exaggerating). Each time noticing something new, and coming up with new questions for the graduate student. For me, this practice and repetition was the only way for me to truly understand the material and become competent enough to make a difference. Over the course of the summer, I continued to deep-dive and build the confidence in my skills and knowledge base to make a difference in this research field.

The culmination of my results led to me publishing a paper “A Scalable and Privacy-Preserving Sealed-Bid Auction framework for IoT Data Marketplaces” and presenting the results at a conference in Houston, Texas. You’ll never guess what I did before I presented. I practiced. I learned a week before this conference that nobody on my team was going to be there. This was my first academic conference, and I was going solo presenting work led by the graduate student. I knew that preparation needed to be done to ensure that I did the paper justice and represented the lab well. I practiced the presentation countless times; had multiple meetings with the grad student to ensure I understood the material and only then did I feel ready. I went to the conference solo, presented solo, and executed at a high standard solo. Practice was important here and just as important to have a team to provide feedback and support me.

Throughout my research career and life, I have learned that practice creates competence, and competence creates confidence. Understanding this principle allows me to understand that confidence can be developed. These skills and experiences have empowered me to be successful in any situation.
