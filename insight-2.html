Everything is a process, and you’re always selling

Within the classroom
MKTG 457: Introduction to Sales
Beyond the classroom
Director of Employer Outreach, CSIET
Concept
The C.A.S.H.U. sales process & roleplay

“Is falling in love a process?”. According to Mr. Craig Westbrook, not only is falling in love a process, but everything is a process. In addition to that, you’re always selling. These concepts were the foundation of Mr. Westbrooks MKTG 457: Introduction to Sales class and something that was reiterated consistently throughout the semester.

In MKTG457, the class was taught the sales process C.A.S.H.U. Connect, Ask, Solve, Handle, and Unite. Connect meant building rapport, spend some time getting to know the client and build trust. Makes sense, you don’t just greet someone walking into your car dealership with “Can you buy my brand new $100,000 car”, it almost always starts with a “Hi, how are you” and further introductions. After introductions and building trust, you then shift to the Ask phase where you are trying to learn more about your customers’ needs and pain points. For example, if continuing with the car example, you could ask, “What type of car are you looking for?”, “What are you driving now?”, “What’s your budget?”. These are all questions that help you learn more about the customer, continue to build trust, and understand what the customer values. For example, an SUV isn’t valuable to someone who wants a sedan. On the flipside, someone whose current car keeps breaking down may value 5-year warranty a little more than someone else without car trouble. This is something you can attack. After understanding the client’s needs and pain points, you can move to the solve phase where you begin to suggest products/solutions that solve their problems. This is where you would begin suggesting to them that brand new 2026 X3, with a 5-year warranty, that is just under their $60,000 budget. At this point, if you are lucky (or did it right), you can move straight to the Unite part and close the deal. Congrats you just made your first commission check! However, it’s usually not that easy. Customers tend to come with objections before buying, and this is where Handle comes in. Handle is for Handle objections. The classic ones are, “This is too expensive”, “I don’t have time right now”, “I need to ask my wife”. It is up to you then to handle these objections and give them a reason why they should buy your product/service instead of walking away. The best way to do this is to handle the objections in the ask phase using questions mentioned above like:” What is your budget?”, “Do you need anybody else here to make this decision” and more. But objections are unavoidable and the goal here is to maintain confidence, preserve trust, and handle these negotiations in faith. If handled properly, you can then unite and proceed with closing the deal or setting up next steps.

C.A.S.H.U is a great sales process, but there is a process to get good at this process. The learning and improvement of sales comes primarily through roleplaying. Roleplaying is the act of changing one’s character to assume someone else’s situation. You commonly work on this with a partner and practice being the seller and buyer. This is another concept that we were taught in MKTG457 as well as primarily in my time beyond the classroom as a member and later Director of Employer Outreach for the Carolina Sales Institute for Engineering & Technology (CSIET). Throughout my time in CSIET, I led employer outreach helping secure 2 partnerships valuing $25,000, as well as plan 3 sales competitions, from sponsors like Microsoft (I placed second in this one), Hoffman & Hoffman, and Nucor. By learning the art of sales and understanding that I am always selling I was able to nurture these client relationships effectively and ultimately close and create real value for our club. Additionally, I understood that everything is a process, whether that was starting cold outreach on LinkedIn or finding numbers with Rocket Reach to cold call. I was always pushing through towards the next step of the process and reaching for the ABCs. Always be Closing.


By breaking down tasks into processes, I have been able to follow repeatable steps, quantify results, and improve effectiveness and efficiency. This framework and way of thinking have greatly contributed to my success as a job interviewee, researcher, software engineer, and solutions consultant. The system is complex, but the processes are doable.
